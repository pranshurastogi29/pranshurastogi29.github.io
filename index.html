    Dual-lens APP-fraud models, from problem statement to production

    In an authorised push-payment (APP) scam, the real customer makes the payment after being tricked, so classic fraud signals like a stolen card or a takeover often never fire. I split the problem into two lenses, one on each side of the payment, and took both models through the full ML lifecycle.

    40%total fraud losses cut
    ProblemAPP scams are authorised by the real customer, so standard fraud signals miss them, and the bank can be liable as both sender and receiver.
    My roleOwned it end to end: problem framing, data and labels, features, both models, deployment and live monitoring.
    ApproachTwo independent gradient-boosted models, one for each side of the payment, using behavioural and graph features with SHAP reason codes.
    ResultTotal fraud losses down 40%, which led to my promotion to Senior.
    Where each lens sits on a scam payment
    Scammer persuades a member
    →
    Member sends the paymentVictim lens
    →
    Account receives the moneyPerpetrator lens
    →
    Funds moved on and cashed out
    Victim lens

    Is this member being scammed right now?

    Scores each outbound payment before it is released.

    • Payee added minutes before paying
    • First payment to this payee
    • Amount far above the member's usual
    • New device or unusual session
    • Scam-style payment reference

    Action: pause the payment and show a warning tailored to the scam pattern, or hold it for review.

    Perpetrator lens

    Is this account receiving scam money?

    Scores accounts as inbound payments arrive.

    • Many inbound payments from unrelated senders
    • Money forwarded on within minutes
    • Young account with little normal activity
    • Linked to flagged accounts by device or details

    Action: restrict the account, stop onward payments and refer it to the financial-crime team.

    Why both sides matter: under the UK's APP reimbursement rules, in force since October 2024, the sending and receiving firms usually split the cost of reimbursing a victim 50:50. A bank can be on the hook as the sender and as the receiver.

      Real-time agentic sanctions screening

      Every transaction is screened against the OFAC, UN, EU and HMT lists, and list updates are applied as they arrive. Fuzzy name matching flags many innocent near-matches. An agent investigates each one the way an analyst would: it pulls the list entry and company records, compares identifiers, then clears the alert with evidence or escalates it.

      95%false-positive alerts removed
      ProblemFuzzy name matching on every transaction flags many innocent near-matches, and each one costs analyst time.
      My roleDesigned and built the real-time agentic screening service over live OFAC, UN, EU and HMT delta feeds.
      ApproachAn LLM agent resolves each near-match by checking identifiers against list entries and company records, then clears it with evidence or escalates it.
      Result95% of false-positive alerts removed, with zero loss of screening coverage.

        Invoice document intelligence

        Business members pay supplier invoices, and fraudsters send edited or fake ones. The engine reads the page with a layout-aware model, extracts every field, and runs specialist agents that each check one thing. Investigators get a ranked evidence report instead of a raw PDF. Select a finding to see where it is on the invoice.

        ProblemFabricated and edited supplier invoices were getting past every rule-based control.

          • EMNLP 2026 · DocInsights
            Consensus Selection over Prompt-Diverse VLM Transcriptions for Expert-Level Document Parsing
            7th on the final leaderboard (Overall 84.54). System description for the Dr.DocBench challenge, DocInsights 2026 shared task. Rastogi P., Tiwari R.

            Parses expert-level book pages (formulas, tables, multi-column layouts, music and chemistry) into Markdown without any training. One vision-language model transcribes each page under several prompts; minimum-Bayes-risk selection, using the challenge's own text and reading-order matching as the utility, picks one transcription per page. PaddleOCR-VL layout rules and a gated word-level consensus refine it. Final scores: Text_ED 0.1641, TEDS 71.86, CDM 81.84, reading order 85.65.
